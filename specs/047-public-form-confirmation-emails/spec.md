# Feature Specification: Public Form Confirmation E-mails

**Feature Branch**: `047-public-form-confirmation-emails`
**Created**: 2026-09-28
**Status**: Implemented and validated 2026-09-28
**Validation State**: Validated. No migration, no new HTTP route, no new feature flag.
**Continuous Workflow Eligible**: Yes.

## Why this spec exists

Three public forms accept a submission and then go silent.

Spec 045 shipped the waiting list, the contact form and the broker application. All three record the
submission, audit it, and answer on screen - and that is the end of it. Spec 045 states the omission
itself: "outbound e-mail for the new forms (waitlist, contact and partner application submit
successfully but send no confirmation e-mail to the visitor or applicant)". The coverage map carried
it as backlog item 7.

`ContactMessagesService.submit` even carried the reason in a comment: notifications are typed by the
`NotificationType` enum in the Prisma schema, contact messages have no value in it, and adding one
would mean a migration. That reasoning explains why the `Notification` path was not used. It does not
justify sending nothing, because the platform already has a second, audited outbound path that needs
no enum and no migration: `RuntimeEmailDeliveryService`, the one the activation and password-reset
messages use.

The consequence is concrete. A visitor who closes the tab keeps no trace of a submission they gave
consent for. A broker who applies has no reference to quote when they follow up, and no written
statement that a licence copy will be requested by e-mail - which is the one thing the public form
cannot collect. And the site has been bilingual since spec 045, so whatever is sent has to be sent in
the language the submitter was reading.

## Constitutional Scope & Compliance (Principle V)

- **Technical platform role**: Unchanged. Each message states that a submission was recorded. It quotes no price, compares no offer, advises nobody and commits no one.
- **Impacted application(s)**: Backend API (notifications, waitlist, contact-messages, partner-applications), shared packages (public-site and partner-application contracts), Web Publique Client (the three forms send the page locale). No back-office and no broker app change.
- **Affected scopes**: The country of the submission appears in the waiting-list message as an ISO code. No product, partner or offer is named.
- **Frontend separation**: Unchanged. The only link in these messages is the public privacy policy, on the public site. No back-office URL is ever sent to a visitor or applicant.
- **Required feature flags**: None new. Delivery follows the existing `EMAIL_SERVICE_TYPE` / `EMAIL_PREVIEW_MODE` configuration, like auth e-mails. With e-mail disabled - the default - every confirmation resolves to `not_configured` and is audited; no submission changes behaviour.
- **Consent and transmission**: All three forms already require an explicit consent literal, which is what makes a transactional acknowledgement to the submitter's own address legitimate. Nothing is transmitted to a third party. The waiting-list message states how to ask for removal.
- **Partner license controls**: Untouched. An application confirmation explicitly states that compliance reviews the file, that no partner account exists, and that a licence copy will be requested - it is neither an acceptance nor a refusal (constitution II: a licence is verified before activation, never assumed).
- **Audit and data history**: Every attempt goes through `RuntimeEmailDeliveryService`, so it is audited under `email.delivery.*` with a masked recipient and its own purpose as target id. Three new purposes keep the three flows distinguishable in that trail.
- **Security and RBAC**: No new route, no new permission. The only recipient is the address the submitter typed, on the submission they just made.
- **Routing impact**: None. No lead, no assignment, no eligibility decision is involved.
- **AI impact**: None. No content is generated by a model.
- **UX/content restrictions**: Every rendered message passes `findForbiddenWording`, and a guardrail test adds the English equivalents (see D3).
- **Data minimisation**: See D1. This is the decision that shapes the feature.
- **Workflow continuity**: Not interrupted.

## Decisions

### D1 - The confirmation carries the reference, never the submitted content

A confirmation repeats nothing the submitter wrote: no message body, no subject, no licence number,
no capacity, no product list. It states that a submission was recorded and quotes its public
reference.

This is spec 044's D1 applied to the other direction of the same problem. An e-mail leaves every
access control the platform applies to that data, it is stored on servers nobody here controls, and
it outlives a consent withdrawal or a retention purge (spec 046 can anonymise the row; it cannot
anonymise a mailbox). The reference is the minimum that makes the message useful - it is what the
submitter quotes when they follow up - and it is already what the on-screen response returns.

The contact message is the clearest case: echoing the body back would duplicate, into an
uncontrolled channel, the exact text spec 046 gave a retention policy.

### D2 - The audited e-mail path, not the `Notification` table

Delivery uses `RuntimeEmailDeliveryService` directly, through a small
`PublicFormNotificationService`, rather than a `Notification` row drained by a worker.

The worker design of spec 044 exists because a lead notification has a lifecycle: it retries, it caps,
it must not send twice, and an operator needs to see a backlog. A form acknowledgement has none of
that. It is worth exactly one attempt at the moment of submission, it has no partner to inform on
failure, and it needs no state. Taking the worker route would mean a new `NotificationType` enum
value, a migration, a second worker cadence and a backlog to monitor - for a message whose only
recovery path is the submitter writing again.

The cost is accepted and bounded: a confirmation that fails is not retried. It is audited under
`email.delivery.failed` and that is where it is read. The submission itself is already stored.

### D3 - Best effort, and never the visitor's problem

The send happens after the row is stored and audited, and cannot change the response. The port
reports `not_configured`, `previewed`, `sent` or `failed`; the wording guardrail refusing a rendering
is a `failed` confirmation, not a failed submission; and each intake service additionally guards
against an unexpected throw. A broken SMTP host must never turn an accepted, consented, recorded
submission into an error screen.

The English copy is reviewed by construction, because the shared `FORBIDDEN_REGULATED_WORDING` list
is French-only. It fully covers the French messages and only catches French wording that leaked into
the English ones. A dedicated guardrail test spells out the English equivalents ("buy now",
"subscribe now", "valid contract", "coverage accepted", "guaranteed callback", "best insurance on the
market", "we recommend"). Widening the shared list itself would re-scan every public surface and
every locale file, which is a separate change with its own blast radius.

### D4 - A duplicate is confirmed exactly like a first submission

The waiting list and the broker application both deduplicate, and both deliberately answer a
duplicate with the same payload as a first submission, so the caller cannot learn that the address
was already known. Staying silent on the duplicate would hand that same fact straight back - to the
one party who owns the address. So both paths send the confirmation, and the application one quotes
the reference already on file, which is the reference the response returns too.

### D5 - The page decides the language, not the browser

The locale travels with the submission as an optional `locale` field on the three create contracts,
narrowed to the two locales the public site serves (`fr`, `en`). The forms read it from the locale the
`[locale]` layout publishes through `NextIntlClientProvider`, not from `Accept-Language`: a visitor
reading the French site is answered in French whatever their browser advertises - the same reason
spec 045 turned `localeDetection` off.

An absent or unsupported value falls back to French rather than failing. The locale is used to pick a
template and is not stored: no column, no migration, and nothing new for spec 046 to anonymise.

## Requirements

- **FR-001** An accepted waiting-list subscription sends a confirmation to the normalised address, naming the country, stating that the registration commits neither party and that no offer was compared, and how to ask for removal.
- **FR-002** An accepted contact message sends an acknowledgement carrying its `CM-` reference, stating that the message will be read and that the acknowledgement is not an answer.
- **FR-003** An accepted broker application sends an acknowledgement carrying its `PA-` reference, stating that compliance reviews the file, that no partner account exists, that a licence copy will be requested by e-mail, and that the message is neither an acceptance nor a refusal.
- **FR-004** No confirmation repeats submitted free text or licence detail (D1).
- **FR-005** Each of the three has its own `EmailPurpose`, so `email.delivery.*` distinguishes them.
- **FR-006** Both locales render; an absent or unsupported locale renders French.
- **FR-007** A refused submission (validation, disabled country, honeypot, rate limit) sends nothing.
- **FR-008** A duplicate sends the same confirmation as a first submission (D4).
- **FR-009** No confirmation outcome can change the HTTP response of a submission (D3).
- **FR-010** Every rendered message passes the forbidden-wording guardrail, in both locales.

## User Scenarios & Testing

1. A visitor joins the waiting list for Senegal from the English site; they receive an English confirmation naming SN, linking `/en/privacy`, and promising nothing.
2. The same visitor joins again; they receive the same confirmation, and the audit trail shows `waitlist.duplicate_ignored` with no second entry.
3. A visitor sends a contact message in French; the acknowledgement carries `CM-2026-...` and contains neither their subject nor their message body.
4. A broker applies; the acknowledgement carries `PA-2026-...`, announces the licence request, and states that no partner account exists.
5. The broker applies a second time with the same licence and address; the acknowledgement quotes the reference already on file.
6. Broker onboarding is disabled for the country; the submission is refused and nothing is sent.
7. A submission carries `locale: "es"`; the request is refused with 400 before anything is written.
8. The mailer is not configured; every submission still succeeds and each attempt is audited as `email.delivery.not_configured`.
9. A template renders regulated wording; the confirmation is `failed`, the submission is not.

## Explicit non-goals

- **No retry and no backlog.** One attempt, audited (D2).
- **No stored locale.** No column, no migration.
- **No widening of the shared forbidden-wording list to English** (D3).
- **No confirmation for the quote journey.** Spec 044 already covers it, through the worker.
- **No unsubscribe link or token.** The waiting-list message asks the submitter to reply; a
  self-service withdrawal endpoint is a change of its own, with its own audit and rate-limit design.
- **No HTML design system.** The bodies are plain paragraphs, like every other transactional message
  in the codebase.
